1. The Problem & Roles

Planning a group trip is exhausting. What starts as an exciting idea quickly devolves into a mess of 150-message WhatsApp threads, disconnected spreadsheets, and scattered map pins. Traditional travel tools fail because open-ended planning causes choice paralysis and puts all administrative burden onto one person.

I built Crewcation to solve this, rescuing the 'Trip Captain' from doing all the work and getting friends out of the group chat and onto the plane. I split the experience into two simple, role-based workflows:

  • The Captain (Organizer): Curates, sets structural trip boundaries (Where, When, Who), unlocks collaborative milestones, and shoulders administrative logistics.
  • The Crew (Guests): Shares preferences anonymously, suggests spots on the map, and casts targeted votes.

2. Consensus & The Vibe Check

Frictionless Onboarding & Invitations

Captains set up the trip and dispatch email invitations. Invited crew members join instantly on the web without app store friction, signing in with Apple, Google, or email verification codes.

Figure 1: Captain's Trip Creation Flow

Figure 2: Crew Onboarding & Invitation Loop

The "Vibe Check" Alignment Suite

Financial mismatch causes tension in over half of group trips. The Vibe Check captures each traveler's budget tier, energy level, and dining preferences once in their profile. Individual budgets stay strictly private; the system aggregates them to calculate destination match scores and align expectations without awkward money conversations.

Map Discovery & Collaborative Crew Notes

A shared map lets anyone suggest places, pin locations, and attach logistics notes directly to spots instead of pasting unorganized links in chat.

The 10-Point Sentiment Slider

To replace polite, unhelpful votes, I designed a nuanced 10-point sentiment scale (from "Not for me" to "I love this!"). Tactile ticks on iOS and a dynamic gradient on web give groups actionable consensus data.

3. How It's Built

Crewcation runs on a single Supabase backend that keeps all trip data in sync across platforms in real time. The frontend ecosystem consists of a native iOS app built in SwiftUI adhering strictly to Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, a responsive Next.js Progressive Web App (PWA) so invited friends can participate instantly without an app install, and a native Android client currently in active development with Jetpack Compose and Material Design 3.

Figma serves as the single source of truth for all design tokens, layouts, and brand assets. To maintain rapid development and strict feature parity as a solo founder, I use autonomous Google Antigravity AI agents to assist with code builds, visual auditing, and cross-platform consistency checks.

7. Results, Impact & Key Portfolio Takeaways

Crewcation shows how a strong design system and simple onboarding can turn a stressful group chore into a fun, collaborative experience.

Key UX Achievements:

  • Low-Friction Signups: Deferring signup until users accept an invite kept onboarding simple and helped groups start planning immediately.
  • Visual Parity: Bringing iOS fluid animations and dynamic styles to React PWA proved that web apps can feel just as premium as native apps.
  • Smart Decision Making: Replacing open-ended chat with suggest-and-vote rounds cut choice paralysis and gave groups a clear path to lock in their plans.
