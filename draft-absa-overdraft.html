Overview

In 2016 Absa wanted customers who already qualified for an overdraft to be able to apply for one in the Absa app. I was the lead UX designer, taking the work from strategy concepts and greyscale wireframes through to final UI with the other designers on the team.

The hard part was telling people they qualified without crowding their balance or pushing credit they didn’t need, so we set one rule early: no dark patterns. We ended up with a full in-app application that starts from one small offer card you can close.

The final home screen. The offer is one card under the greeting, above the accounts, and the X closes it.

An overdraft offer can do real harm

Credit is a sensitive product. An overdraft is a useful safety net, but nudging people into one when they don’t need it is unethical and bad for their finances. So the design question was never how to drive applications. It was this:

How do we tell qualifying customers about their overdraft without cluttering the account screen, hiding their balance or encouraging credit they don’t need?

We ruled out the loud options

The obvious way to sell an overdraft is a full-screen prompt, or something that flashes. We took those off the table early. Instead we worked with small “decorations” on the home and account screens, plus one place where every offer lives.

It’s a trade: some people will never notice the offer. We accepted that, because the balance had to stay the most important thing on the screen.

Five ways to say “you qualify”

We tried several ways to place the offer on the account screen: a badge that opens when you tap it, a strip under the header, a tab on the balance card and a bar inside it. Each had to be visible when someone checked their balance and easy to ignore when they didn’t care.

We also explored messages you could dismiss for now or for good, and an offer card that only shows up when the balance drops below a set level.

1. A small badge on the balance header

1. Tapped open: “You qualify for R20,000.00”

2. A strip under the header

3. A tab on the balance card

4. A bar inside the balance card

In every concept the balance stays the biggest thing on the screen. The offer is a badge, a strip or a bar beside it.

Offers live where people look for offers

Not everyone wants to be told. So every pre-approved product also sits on one page, My Offers, reached from the All about me menu. It lists the overdraft next to a credit limit increase and a personal loan. Someone who’s curious can go and look, and nobody has to be interrupted for it to be found.

My Offers in greyscale (left) and final UI (right): the pre-approved overdraft, a credit limit increase and a pre-approved loan in one list.

The responsible questions come first

The application opens by asking if you’re insolvent or under debt counselling, now or in the past. After that, each short step asks you to confirm one thing (your income, your deductions, your fixed debts) with a Change details button underneath. The terms screen covers spousal consent in plain words.

We built all of it in greyscale first, which let us sort out field order and button placement on the iPhone 7 before any visual design.

The greyscale flow

Eleven screens, from home to the referral screen. Tap a screen to see what it does.

Every state had a screen, including the wait

The final UI moved the greyscale structure into Absa’s visual style. We gave the moments in between as much care as the forms. Surecheck sends a verification to your registered phone and counts down 60 seconds. A processing screen covers the check itself, and the approval screen tells you your funds are available.

If an application needed a person to look at it, the customer got a reference number and a consultant followed up.

What we can say about the result

I don’t have a number for this feature on its own.

What we delivered was a complete overdraft application inside the app, with one closable offer card and a single offers page instead of pop-ups. It was part of the wider Absa app redesign, which reached a 4-star average on both app stores and reduced calls to the call centre. Those are results for the whole app, not this one feature.
