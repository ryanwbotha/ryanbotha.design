Group trips usually die in the group chat. Crewcation is my own product: an app that gets a group of friends from "we should go somewhere" to an actual plan. One person sets up the trip, and everyone else shares what they want in private and votes on places. I designed it and built it on my own, with AI agents doing much of the coding and Figma as the source of truth, and it's live on iOS and the web, with Android in development.

Home, a city's places, then the slider mid-vote. A trip goes from idea to decision in three screens.

1. Group trips die in the chat

A group trip starts as a great idea and turns into a 150-message thread, a spreadsheet nobody updates and map pins scattered across five phones. One person ends up doing all the admin, and open-ended planning leaves everyone stuck. So I split the work into two roles. The Captain sets where, when and who, and moves the trip forward. The Crew shares preferences privately, suggests places and votes.

Create > Invite > Suggest > Vote > Finalize.

The old way ends in decision fatigue. Crewcation turns the same group into five steps that end in a plan.

2. Friends can say yes without downloading anything

This was the biggest call I made. The Captain cares enough to install an app. The Crew mostly wants to see the plan and say yes. So the invite email opens the trip in a web app, and you only sign up (Apple, Google or an email code) once you've accepted. Building a second client as a solo founder is expensive. I took that cost on purpose, because every extra step at the invite is a chance for a friend to drift off.

The Captain picks single, multi-stop or road trip first. Curated trips arrive with their stops already built in.

Notice the fork. Existing users see the invite on their home screen, everyone else gets an email that opens on the web.

3. Nobody has to say their budget out loud

Money is the awkward part of a group trip. It's easier to go along with a plan you can't afford than to be the one who says so. The Vibe Check asks each traveller three things, once: energy, eating style and budget tier. Budgets are never shown to anyone else. The app combines them into a match score for each destination, so the group sees what fits without anyone naming a number.

One question per screen on iOS: chill or thrill, how you like to eat, and a budget tier from $ to $$$$.

On the web the same three questions sit together in settings, so they're quick to change later.

4. Ideas go on a map, then get a real vote

Links pasted into a chat get lost. In Crewcation anyone can pin a place to a shared map, filter by Stay, Do or Eat, and leave crew notes on the place itself. Then everyone rates it. A thumbs-up can't tell "sure, fine" from "I really want this", so I designed a 10-point slider from "Not for me" to "I love this!". On iOS each step gives a light haptic tick.

Filter, search the area, open a place. Crew notes and "Assign to day" live on the place, not in the chat.

5. One designer, three platforms, one source of truth

Stack: one Supabase backend, SwiftUI for iOS, Next.js for the web app, Jetpack Compose for Android (in progress).

Figma holds every token, layout and asset. Agents in Google Antigravity do much of the coding, and they check each build against Figma and against the other platforms. I don't ask for pixel copies. A date picker should feel native on iPhone and Android and light on the web. AI gets me to working code fast. My eye decides what ships.

One backend keeps every trip in sync in real time, whichever app a friend happens to use.

6. Next: settling up without the awkward IOU

Money comes back at the end of a trip, so the next feature is an expense splitter, now in development. Snap a receipt, pick a photo or type it in. Gemini reads the receipt and fills in the amount, place and category. The app works out who owes whom and writes the request for you to post in the trip chat. It started as a sketch on paper.

The first sketch. Note the default: AI reads the receipt and splits it with the whole crew, and you untick anyone who wasn't there.

8. What shipped

Crewcation is live on iOS and the web, and Android is in development. I did the research, design and build myself, from paper sketches to the live apps. What I'd point to:

  • Joining is quick. Friends open the invite on the web and sign up only after they accept, so a group can start planning the day the trip is created.
  • Decisions have a path. Suggest-and-vote rounds replace open-ended chat, and the slider shows how strongly people feel, not only whether they agree.
  • The web app holds up next to native. It carries the iOS app's motion and polish from one shared design system.
