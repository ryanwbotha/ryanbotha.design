What I took from it

Built around the deal, not the form

This is one piece of the EnPowered Financing Accelerator. Its first two versions were wizards: one question per screen, a summary at the end. That got partners started, but it fought them every time they needed to change something. I restructured the product around the deal instead of the form, with tabbed sections partners could move between freely and a real-time summary of the results next to the inputs.

The wizard was the right call for the MVP

A wizard was a good first move. It turned a dense loan spreadsheet into a short run of plain questions and gave partners a clean proposal at the end. Calls with partners and the usage data told us it worked. They also showed us where it stopped working.

The Typeform MVP. One field, one screen, and no sense of the whole deal.

Every change meant walking back through the steps

Financing is a what-if exercise. What if the incentive lands later? What if the term is longer? In a wizard, each of those questions means stepping back through the flow, and the end of the flow is a review of what you typed. The financial results only came back after you submitted. Partners couldn't see an input and its effect on the same screen.

Before: the last step reviewed the inputs. The money answers came later.

I mapped the product around the deal before drawing screens

I started with a flow map, not layouts. Two hubs came out of it: a list of projects (later called deals) and a summary page for each one. Everything partners actually do hangs off those two pages: share, export, preview the report, request financing, edit and save. The form stopped being the product and became one way to fill in a deal.

The IA map. Two blue hubs, list and summary, with every action branching from them.

The simplest layout won

I sketched several layouts, including one with an info panel beside the form. I kept the simplest: the same sections the wizard already had, now as tabs down the left. Partners didn't have to relearn the content, only how to move through it.

Sketching the left-hand tabs. The section names carried straight over from the wizard.

Inputs and results on one screen

In the shipped version, the Cost & Savings tab shows the inputs at the top and the first-year estimates and payment schedule right underneath. Change the utility costs or the escalator and you see what it does to the deal straight away. The projects list means any deal can be picked up again later, which a wizard never allowed.

Structure should follow the thing people change

Wizards are good at starting things and poor at changing them. When a task is going to be revisited, I build the structure around the object being changed, not the order it was first entered in.
