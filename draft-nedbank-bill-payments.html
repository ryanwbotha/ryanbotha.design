Bills shouldn't depend on memory

Paying recurring bills in the Nedbank app meant remembering each one: the municipality, the insurer, the store card. Bill payments lets people load a bill once. Nedbank fetches it and pays it on the day they choose, from the card they choose, and lets them know every time.

I was the product design lead. I worked with a junior UX designer and a junior UI designer: I set the direction, handled the stakeholders and mentored both of them through the work. We designed it for iOS, Android, mobile web, tablet and desktop.

The Pay menu, then the first two onboarding screens: less admin, paid bills, and load your bill once.

Find the biller, not the form

Setting up a bill starts with who you're paying. People search an A to Z list of service providers, from municipalities to insurers, instead of typing bank details. Then they add the reference they already have on their bill, an account number or an EasyPay number, choose scheduled or once-off, and give it a name they'll remember.

Pick a service provider, choose the type of reference, then add it with a name you'll recognize later.

Automatic, but with a ceiling

Automatic payment is the point, but a surprise bill shouldn't empty someone's account. When people turn auto payment on, they set a maximum amount. If a bill comes in higher, we let them know. People who'd rather check each bill can choose manual: we fetch it on the day they pick and wait for their approval.

Auto payment asks for a maximum amount. Manual payment fetches the bill on a chosen day and waits for approval.

Small rules prevent big mistakes

A lot of the design lived in short notes at the point of choice. Pick the 31st and the bill is paid on the last day of shorter months. Choose a payment day and you're reminded to leave enough money in the account. The card list shows only active cards the biller accepts, and an Amex card shows the double Greenbacks it earns.

Each note answers the question people would otherwise have to call about.

Security without a dead end

Every new biller is confirmed with Approve-it, Nedbank's in-app approval, with a countdown on screen. If the message doesn't arrive, a one-time PIN by SMS takes over, so nobody gets stuck halfway. Afterwards each bill has its own page with its details and payment history.

Approve-it with a countdown and an SMS fallback, then a page for each bill.

Leading a small team

I directed the work and made the design calls with the two designers. I took the stakeholder conversations and mentored both of them as we went. Together we carried one flow across every platform the bank supports, from iOS and Android to mobile web, tablet and desktop.
