Taste Still Matters
Why I'm not becoming a plumber

Type

Writing

Published

October 2026

Topic

Design, AI, Taste

The other day I read an essay by Dennis Brotzky about Wealthsimple's first year after the acquisition, and how fast his team has been building a great banking product with the help of AI. It inspired me, and honestly, it relieved me.

Lately I've been hearing a lot about my career as a designer, and how it's a profession AI is going to make obsolete. Some people have said this to me in person. That scared me. But a few chats with good connections (thanks Larry Sawyer!) helped me see the truth, or at least gave me hope that I still have a future as a designer, and that I don't need to start training as a plumber any time soon. (No dig at plumbers. It's just not my thing.)

The part of Dennis' essay that stood out most to me was what he said about taste. AI agents are amazing, and they can build almost anything you ask. But they often don't follow instructions, they build features nobody needs, and a lot of the time what they make is ugly. That can be subjective, but most good designers know when a design is bad.

You could try to hand all your design and engineering to AI. But I wholeheartedly agree with Dennis:

"You still need to know what to ask for, and a high level of taste to know when an answer is wrong, or right but not good. The agent multiplies whatever judgment you bring to it. It's a tool with incredible leverage that rewards agency and taste." Dennis Brotzky, Wealthsimple: one year post-acquisition

That's how I work now. I use AI to get to a working prototype in days instead of weeks, and I keep it in greyscale at first so I focus on the flow before the polish. AI makes the prototype fast. Deciding what to build and how it should feel is still my job.

Thanks to everyone who keeps pushing for beautiful design and hasn't given up the fight for good taste. At least for now, we still have skin in the game.
